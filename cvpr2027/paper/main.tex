\begin{abstract}
A likelihood-based \ransac score can integrate out the inlier noise scale
while still requiring an outlier half-width in residual units. We estimate
this half-width from the upper tail of absolute residual coordinates.
A generalized Pareto fit supplies either the endpoint of a uniform
outlier distribution or both the endpoint and shape of a nonuniform
distribution. Both scores preserve hypothesis rankings under a common
change of residual units with Jeffreys' scale prior and an equivariant
integration grid for soft labels. Synthetic experiments show that
calibrating the uniform half-width can compensate for a misspecified
inlier probability. Integrating over that probability improves inlier
classification when inliers are rare, but hard assignments become
unstable under heavy overlap. Soft assignments improve F1 in that
regime. When outlier shape and extent vary across trials, the soft
generalized Pareto model achieves the highest mean F1 among the tested scores.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A geometric hypothesis can agree with an incorrect correspondence by
chance. How much that agreement counts in \ransac~\cite{Fischler1981}
depends on both the localization error of an inlier and the probability
that an outlier lies close to the model. The profile-marginal score
of Pritts et~al.~\cite{Pritts2026} integrates out the Gaussian inlier
scale for each candidate partition. Its uniform outlier component still
requires a half-width $a$ in residual units. Increasing $a$ lowers the
outlier density and makes a small residual stronger evidence for an inlier.

We estimate $a$ from the largest absolute residual coordinates. Under
the uniform model, each coordinate magnitude has upper endpoint $a$.
A peaks-over-threshold fit~\cite{Balkema1974,Pickands1975,Coles2001}
estimates this endpoint using a dimensionless quantile level. We compare
two uses of the fit. The first retains a uniform density over the
estimated support. The second also uses the fitted shape to model
variation in outlier density within that support.

Estimating the half-width exposes the role of the inlier probability
$\gamma$. For partitions covered by the uniform support, calibrating
$a$ can compensate for fixing $\gamma$ at the wrong value. We derive
this equivalence and compare fixing $\gamma$ with integrating it out.
Synthetic trials isolate how these choices affect inlier classification
when the two populations overlap or the outlier distribution varies.

\paragraph{Extreme values in sampling and scoring.}
\evsac~\cite{Fragoso2013evsac} uses second-nearest descriptor distances
to fit a generalized extreme-value model of the closest incorrect match.
Together with a Gamma model for correct matches, this yields weights
for sampling minimal sets. Our fit uses the upper tail of a geometric
residual under a candidate hypothesis. It acts during scoring and can
therefore be combined with \evsac or \prosac~\cite{ChumMatas2005}.

\paragraph{Geometric scoring.}
Inlier counting and \msac~\cite{Torr1998} require a distance tolerance.
\mlesac~\cite{Torr2000} evaluates a Gaussian--uniform mixture at a
specified inlier scale and fits the inlier probability by expectation
maximization~\cite{Dempster1977}. A fixed Gaussian quantile relates
scale to tolerance~\cite{Shekhovtsov2025}. \magsac and
\magsacpp~\cite{Barath2019,Barath2020} integrate over scales up to a
prescribed maximum. \assc, \askc, \simfit and
\simfitpp~\cite{Wang2004,Wang2010,Heinrich2013,Edstedt2025} estimate
scale from residuals. Other methods use agreement between residual
rankings~\cite{Raguram2011} or learn scores with or without
correspondences~\cite{Barath2022,BarrosoLaguna2023}.

Our formulation builds on outlier-process models of robust
estimation~\cite{BlackRangarajan1996} and Bayesian treatments of mixture
parameters~\cite{Torr2002,Pritts2026}. A-contrario methods such as
\minpran~\cite{Stewart1995} and \orsa/\acransac~\cite{Moisan2004,Moisan2012,Moulon2012}
quantify chance agreement under a specified background model. Here the
residuals of each hypothesis determine the extent of its outlier component.

\section{Residual model}
\label{sec:method}

\label{sec:model}

Consider $N$ observations $\bx_i \in \Reals^d$, $i = 1,\ldots,N$, and
a candidate geometric model~$\btheta \in \Reals^{n_\theta}$. The
noise-free point~$\bar\bx_i$ of each inlier lies on the model manifold
$\cM_{\btheta} = \{\bx \in \Reals^d : \bg(\bx,\btheta) = \bzero\}$,
whose codimension $d_g = d - \dim\cM_{\btheta}$ is the number of
directions normal to it.

An inlier is a noisy observation of a point on the model,
$\bx_i = \bar\bx_i + \bvarepsilon_i$ with $\bg(\bar\bx_i,\btheta) = \bzero$
and $\bvarepsilon_i \sim \NN(\bzero, \sigma^2\btSigma_{\bx_i})$, where the
shape $\btSigma_{\bx_i}$ is known and the inlier scale~$\sigma$ is
not. To first order $\bg_i \coloneqq \bg(\bx_i,\btheta) \approx
\bJ_i\bvarepsilon_i$, with $\bJ_i$ the Jacobian of~$\bg$ with respect
to~$\bx$. Whitening by $\bL_i\bL_i^\top = \bJ_i\btSigma_{\bx_i}\bJ_i^\top$
gives the residual
\begin{equation}
 \br_i \coloneqq \bL_i^{-1}\bg_i \;\sim\; \NN(\bzero, \sigma^2\bI_{d_g}),
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
whose squared norm $\delta_i^2 = \|\br_i\|^2 =
\bg_i^\top(\bJ_i\btSigma_{\bx_i}\bJ_i^\top)^{-1}\bg_i$ is the general
form of the squared Sampson
distance~\cite{Sampson1982,Hartley2003,Chojnacki2000}. Equivalently,
it is the squared Mahalanobis distance, with respect
to~$\btSigma_{\bx_i}$, from~$\bx_i$ to the linearization
of~$\cM_{\btheta}$ at~$\bx_i$. For analyses of this approximation,
see~\cite{Harker2006,Rydell2024}. Marginalizing the unknown true point
along the manifold makes the density of the residual along any ray
normal to it Gaussian for Gaussian observation noise, in any codimension,
and close to Gaussian otherwise~\cite[Sec.~3.2]{Shekhovtsov2025}.
Empirical inlier residuals on
PhotoTourism and HPatches support a Gaussian approximation, with
variation in scale across keypoints~\cite[Fig.~3]{Shekhovtsov2025}.
Similar behavior has been reported across relative-pose
matchers~\cite{Edstedt2025}.

We adopt the standard \gaussunif
mixture~\cite{Torr2000,Shekhovtsov2025}: a latent label
$z_i \in \{0,1\}$ marks observation~$i$ as inlier ($z_i{=}1$) or outlier
($z_i{=}0$) with prior $\Pr(z_i{=}1) = \gamma$, and the
residual~$\br_i$ has conditional density
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 p(\br_i \mid z_i{=}1, \btheta, \sigma) &= \pin(\br_i;\sigma)
   \coloneqq (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-d_g/2}\,e^{-\|\br_i\|^2/2\sigma^2},\\
 p(\br_i \mid z_i{=}0, \btheta, a) &= p_a(\br_i) \coloneqq (2a)^{-d_g}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\|\br_i\|_\infty\le a\}},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:component-densities}
\end{equation}
where $a$ is the half-width of the outlier cube in residual units. A
random mismatch has unrelated components, so its density is uniform over
the observation domain up to the domain's
boundary~\cite[Sec.~3.2.2]{Shekhovtsov2025}; the boundary is where the
generalized Pareto component of \cref{sec:gpd-component} departs from
the uniform one.
The earlier score fixes $a$ after normalizing by image
extent~\cite{Pritts2026}. We estimate it from residual coordinates.

Collecting the inlier labels defines a \tinlier set
$\cI \coloneqq \{i : z_i = 1\}$, with complement
$\cO = \{1,\ldots,N\}\setminus\cI$ of \toutliers, $n_I = |\cI|$ and
$n_O = |\cO|$. By conditional independence, the residual likelihood conditional on the partition is
\begin{equation}
 p(\br\mid\btheta,\cI,\sigma,a)
 =\prod_{i\in\cI}\pin(\br_i;\sigma)
  \prod_{i\in\cO}p_a(\br_i).
 \label{eq:partition-likelihood}
\end{equation}
With $\gamma=\tfrac12$, all partitions have prior mass $2^{-N}$.
\Cref{sec:label-prior} gives the partition prior for unknown $\gamma$.

\subsection{The inlier component}
\label{sec:evpm}
\label{sec:inlier}

At a fixed partition, the inlier scale can be integrated out
analytically~\cite{Pritts2026}.

Write $\RSS_\cI=\sum_{i\in\cI}\delta_i^2$ and
$\nu=n_I d_g-n_\theta$. The latter is the residual degrees-of-freedom
adjustment used in the earlier score for a fitted model. With the variance prior $\sigma^2\sim\IG(\alpha_0,\beta_0)$, its adjusted inlier term
is, up to a common additive constant,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 A(\cI)={}&\log\Gamma(\nu/2+\alpha_0)-\tfrac{\nu}{2}\log(2\pi)\\
 &-(\nu/2+\alpha_0)\log(\RSS_\cI/2+\beta_0).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:evpm}
\end{equation}
In the limit of Jeffreys' prior~\cite{Jeffreys1946}, $\alpha_0=\beta_0=0$ and
$\pi(\sigma^2)\propto1/\sigma^2$; the integral requires $\nu>0$
and $\RSS_\cI>0$.

\subsection{Uniform outliers with an estimated half-width}
\label{sec:outlier}\label{sec:why-maxima}

The uniform outlier model assigns residual vectors to the cube
$[-a,a]^{d_g}$. Its half-width is a coordinate bound. A vector near a
corner can have norm as large as $\sqrt{d_g}\,a$, so fitting the upper
tail of the norms would generally target the wrong endpoint. We instead
pool the absolute coordinates of the \toutliers, retaining
multiplicities:
\begin{equation}
 \cZ(\cO)=\bigl(|r_{ij}|:i\in\cO,\ j=1,\ldots,d_g\bigr).
 \label{eq:pooled}
\end{equation}
Under the uniform model, each absolute coordinate is uniform on $[0,a]$.

\paragraph{Choice of tail.}
For a block of $b$ independent uniform magnitudes, the expected
minimum is $a/(b+1)$. This suggests estimating $a$ from block minima,
but a short prefix leaves genuine inliers in its complement. Their
small residuals depress the estimated half-width and inflate the
uniform density level. Upper-tail estimation is less sensitive to
these residuals when gross errors dominate the tail. We use peaks
over threshold to retain all exceedances and compare with block
extremes in \cref{sec:synthetic}.

Choose a quantile level $\eta$ and set $u=Q_\eta(\cZ(\cO))$.
For $z>u$, the excess is $y=z-u$. Under the usual extreme-value
domain-of-attraction conditions, a generalized Pareto distribution
approximates the excess distribution at a sufficiently high
threshold~\cite{Balkema1974,Pickands1975}. Its distribution function is
\begin{equation}
 G_u(y)=1-(1+\xi y/\omega)^{-1/\xi},
 \label{eq:gpd}
\end{equation}
for $y\geq0$, $1+\xi y/\omega>0$ and $\omega>0$, with the
exponential limit at $\xi=0$. A negative shape $\xi$ gives the finite
endpoint $u-\omega/\xi$. For a uniform coordinate magnitude the excess is
exactly uniform on $[0,a-u]$, so $\xi=-1$ and $\omega=a-u$.

We fit the shape and scale of the excesses by probability-weighted
moments~\cite{Hosking1987}. For a finite-endpoint fit, the estimated
half-width and corresponding uniform density level are
\begin{align}
 \ahat(\cO)&=u-\hat\omega/\hat\xi,  \label{eq:endpoint}\\
 \pout(\cO)&=(2\ahat(\cO))^{-d_g}.     \label{eq:pout}
\end{align}
We compute $u$ by linear interpolation. With fewer than five excesses
or $\hat\xi\geq-10^{-3}$, we use the largest coordinate magnitude in
the complement. This excludes unbounded fits and finite endpoints that
are unstable near zero shape. Otherwise we raise the fitted endpoint
to the observed maximum if necessary to cover the complement.

\subsection{Generalized Pareto outliers}
\label{sec:gpd-component}

The second component extends the fitted generalized Pareto law down
to zero. Threshold stability describes excesses above higher thresholds
and cannot establish this extension from the tail alone. Under this
additional assumption, the density of an absolute outlier coordinate is
\begin{equation}
 f(z;\hat\xi,\ahat)=\frac{1}{\omega_0}
 \Bigl(1+\frac{\hat\xi z}{\omega_0}\Bigr)^{-1/\hat\xi-1},
 \quad 0\leq z<\ahat,\quad \omega_0=|\hat\xi|\,\ahat,
 \label{eq:gpd-density}
\end{equation}
and, with independent coordinates and symmetric signs, the outlier
density is $p(\br_i)=\prod_j\tfrac12 f(|r_{ij}|)$, which replaces $p_a$ in
\cref{eq:partition-likelihood}. At $\hat\xi=-1$, $f=1/\ahat$ and this
is the uniform component: the \gaussunif score is the special case
$\xi=-1$ of a Gaussian--generalized-Pareto mixture, so the two models are
nested. For $-1<\hat\xi<0$ the density falls toward the endpoint, and its
value at the model, $f(0)=1/(|\hat\xi|\ahat)$, exceeds the uniform
level.

\paragraph{Coordinate and radial densities.}

For $d_g=1$ the absolute coordinate is the distance. For $d_g\geq2$ the
two differ. Under isotropy, a density $f(\delta)$ on distances defines the residual
density $f(\delta)/(S_{d_g-1}\delta^{d_g-1})$, where $S_{d_g-1}$ is the
area of the unit sphere. If $f(0)>0$, as for a generalized Pareto law
carried down to zero, this residual density diverges at the model for
$d_g\geq2$, and the resulting likelihood can favor outliers arbitrarily close to the model. The norm
of a uniform vector in the cube also has a density that vanishes toward the
maximum norm ($\xi=-\tfrac12$ for $d_g=2$), so a model on distances would
read the geometry of the cube as thinning outliers. On coordinates the
uniform cube has $\xi=-1$ exactly. This is the distinction Shekhovtsov
draws between the density of the residual along a ray normal to the
manifold, which defines the likelihood, and the distribution of the
residual's norm, which does not~\cite[Sec.~5.1]{Shekhovtsov2025}.

\paragraph{Endpoint.}
For $-1<\hat\xi<0$ the density vanishes at the endpoint, so the largest
coordinate cannot lie on it. Where the fitted endpoint does not exceed
the largest coordinate $z_{(n)}$, the component uses $z_{(n)}$ plus the
mean spacing of the complement's coordinates, which is positive unless
they all tie and is also equivariant under a change of units.

\subsection{The inlier probability}
\label{sec:label-prior}

With a general prior $\gamma$, the label prior adds
$n_I\log\gamma+n_O\log(1-\gamma)$ to the score. At a fixed width,
changing $\gamma$ to $\gamma'$ changes every partition's score by
$n_I\log\frac{\gamma'(1-\gamma)}{\gamma(1-\gamma')}$ plus a constant,
and changing $a$ to $a'$ changes it by $n_I d_g\log(a'/a)$ plus a
constant. For partitions covered by both cubes, the scores at
$(\gamma',a)$ and at $(\gamma,a')$ differ by a common constant when
\begin{equation}
 a'=a\left(\frac{\gamma'/(1-\gamma')}{\gamma/(1-\gamma)}\right)^{1/d_g}.
 \label{eq:gamma-width}
\end{equation}
Thus calibration of the uniform density level can absorb a misspecified
inlier probability. A change in support coverage also affects the
indicator in \cref{eq:component-densities}.

The earlier score noted that $\gamma$ can be integrated out under its
conjugate prior~\cite{Pritts2026}. Under $\gamma\sim\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_\gamma,\beta_\gamma)$,
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^1\gamma^{n_I}(1-\gamma)^{n_O}\,\pi(\gamma)\,d\gamma
 =\frac{B(n_I+\alpha_\gamma,\,n_O+\beta_\gamma)}{B(\alpha_\gamma,\beta_\gamma)},
 \label{eq:label-marginal}
\end{equation}
and its logarithm depends only on the inlier count. It adds constant
work per prefix and preserves invariance to residual units. We use
Jeffreys' prior on $\gamma$, with $\alpha_\gamma=\beta_\gamma=\tfrac12$.
We also compare profiling $\gamma$ at $n_I/N$.

\section{Hypothesis scoring}
\label{sec:inference}

We compare selecting a residual-norm prefix (hard labels) with
summing over labels conditional on the mixture parameters (soft labels).
In the tables, \stuU denotes a supplied uniform half-width, \stuUev an
estimated uniform half-width, and \stuGP a fitted generalized Pareto
density. The suffix $\beta$ denotes integration over $\gamma$, and the
prefix soft- denotes label summation.

\subsection{Hard labels by prefix selection}
\label{sec:computation}

With the uniform outlier component of \cref{sec:outlier}, the score of
a partition is
\begin{equation}
 S(\btheta,\cI)=A(\cI)-n_O d_g\log\bigl(2\ahat(\cO)\bigr).
 \label{eq:evpm-closed}
\end{equation}
This expression uses the uniform density level in \cref{eq:pout};
its interpretation as a log-likelihood assumes support coverage.
Supplying a fixed $a$ recovers the earlier score. With the
generalized Pareto component, the outlier term is
$\sum_{i\in\cO}\log p(\br_i)$ from \cref{sec:gpd-component}, and with
$\gamma$ integrated out the score gains the logarithm of
\cref{eq:label-marginal}.

Sort the observations by $\delta_i$. For each admissible prefix
$\cI_k=\{(1),\ldots,(k)\}$, accumulate its sum of squares, fit the
upper tail of its complement, and evaluate \cref{eq:evpm-closed}.
For each prefix we use the chosen outlier term and label prior. The hypothesis score is
\begin{equation}
 S^*(\btheta)=\max_{k}S(\btheta,\cI_k).
 \label{eq:evpm-profile}
\end{equation}
Thus we marginalize the inlier scale and profile over the prefix length;
we call $S^*$ the \emph{extreme-value profile-marginal score}. The
search covers all partitions induced by a threshold on residual norm.
For a fixed uniform cube covering all observations, a prefix is optimal at each size because
exchanging an inlier for a closer observation reduces $\RSS_\cI$.
With a fitted width, the exchange can also change $\ahat$. The earlier
argument therefore gives no guarantee of optimality over arbitrary
subsets.

\subsection{Soft labels by mixture integration}
\label{sec:soft}

Conditional on a shared scale and inlier probability, summing each
label gives the mixture likelihood~\cite{Torr2000}. We fit the
outlier density $p$ once to all coordinate magnitudes for the
hypothesis, assuming outliers dominate the upper tail.
Integrating over the shared parameters gives the plug-in score

\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 p(\br\mid\btheta)={}&\iint\prod_{i=1}^{N}
 \bigl[\gamma\,\pin(\br_i;\sigma)+(1-\gamma)\,p(\br_i)\bigr]\\
 &\times\pi(\sigma^2)\,\pi(\gamma)\,d\sigma^2\,d\gamma,
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:soft}
\end{equation}
The all-outlier term in the product is independent of $\sigma$.
Consequently the integral under an unbounded Jeffreys prior diverges
whenever that term is positive. The numerical score uses a finite grid
uniform in $\log\sigma$, corresponding to a truncated log-uniform prior.
Grid bounds are set relative to the median residual and scale with the
residual units. We use Beta-weighted cells for $\gamma$ or evaluate at
$\gamma=\tfrac12$. To classify observations, we average each conditional
inlier probability using the normalized posterior grid weights and
select observations with probability at least $\tfrac12$.

\subsection{Properties}
\label{sec:properties}

\begin{proposition}[Invariance to residual units]
\label{prop:invariance}
Under Jeffreys' prior, multiplying all residuals by $c>0$ preserves the
maximizing prefixes and the ranking of hypotheses of the same model class,
provided admissibility is unchanged by a change of units.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Residual order is unchanged and $\RSS_\cI$ scales by $c^2$.
The quantile, excesses and fitted scale all scale by $c$, while the
fitted shape is dimensionless. Hence $\ahat$ scales by $c$. The total
change in \cref{eq:evpm-closed} is
$-(\nu+n_Od_g)\log c=-(Nd_g-n_\theta)\log c$, common to every
prefix and hypothesis.
\end{proof}
The same argument covers \cref{sec:gpd-component,sec:label-prior}: the
generalized Pareto density of \cref{eq:gpd-density} scales by $1/c$,
exactly like the uniform level, and the label term has no units.
For the soft score, rescaling the finite grid multiplies the mixture likelihood by $c^{-Nd_g}$ at every cell, preserving the ranking. With an informative scale prior, $\beta_0$ must also be expressed in the new
squared-distance units. The earlier score likewise requires conversion
of its supplied width $a$; here the endpoint fit performs that
conversion automatically.

\paragraph{Quantile stability.}
If excesses above $u_0$ follow a generalized Pareto law with parameters
$(\xi,\omega_0)$, raising the threshold to $u$ within its support gives
the same shape and scale $\omega_0+\xi(u-u_0)$~\cite{Coles2001}. For
$\xi<0$, the new endpoint is still $u_0-\omega_0/\xi$. Thus $\eta$
changes how much data enter the fit while preserving the population
endpoint under the tail model. Finite-sample estimates can change:
a higher threshold leaves fewer excesses, and a lower one may extend
below the range where the approximation holds.

\paragraph{Interpreting a selected partition.}
At fixed width and scale, equating Gaussian and uniform densities gives
the equal-density radius, when the logarithm is nonnegative,
\begin{equation}
 \tau=\hat s\sqrt{2d_g\log\!\left(\frac{\ahat}{\hat s}
                         \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\right)},
 \label{eq:implicit-threshold}
\end{equation}
where $\hat s^2=(\RSS_\cI+2\beta_0)/(\nu+2\alpha_0)$.
This gives a reference radius within the uniform support for equal label priors. The discrete
prefix decision in \cref{eq:evpm-profile} also accounts for the change
in the fitted width between consecutive prefixes.

\subsection{Complexity}
\label{sec:complexity}

\Cref{tab:complexity} gives costs after residual computation. For a
supplied uniform half-width, a fixed-pass radix sort of 32-bit residual
keys and a sweep of prefix sums each cost $\Theta(N)$~\cite{Pritts2026}.
The inlier probability contributes constant work per prefix.

\paragraph{Uniform outliers with hard labels.}
For $d_g=1$ the complement of the prefix $\cI_k$ is the suffix
$z_{(k+1)},\ldots,z_{(N)}$ of the sorted distances, and its upper tail is
a suffix of that suffix. The threshold $u$ is an order statistic, or the
interpolation of two, at a known rank, and the first value above it is
found by bisection. The probability-weighted-moment fit needs only the
number $m$ of excesses, their sum, and the rank-weighted sum
$\sum_j j\,y_{(j)}$, and with the suffix sums
$T_1(g)=\sum_{l\geq g}z_{(l)}$ and $T_2(g)=\sum_{l\geq g}(N-l+1)\,z_{(l)}$,
which rank from the top so that a short tail's moments never come from a
difference of two large sums,
\begin{equation}
 \textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{m} j\,z_{(N-m+j)} = (m+1)\,T_1(g)-T_2(g),
 \qquad g=N-m+1,
 \label{eq:pwm-prefix}
\end{equation}
with the shift by $u$ applied in closed form. Each prefix therefore costs
$O(\log N)$, and the sweep $O(N\log N)$ after the $\Theta(N)$ sort. For
$d_g\geq2$ the pooled coordinates need their own sort, and removing the
next vector deletes $d_g$ coordinates from arbitrary ranks. A segment
tree over the sorted coordinates whose nodes hold a count, a sum and a
rank-weighted sum, merged as
$R=R_{\mathrm{left}}+R_{\mathrm{right}}+n_{\mathrm{left}}\,s_{\mathrm{right}}$,
deletes a coordinate and returns the moments of the top $m$ in
$O(\log(Nd_g))$.

\paragraph{Generalized Pareto outliers with hard labels.}
The outlier term $\sum_{i\in\cO}\log p(\br_i)$ depends on the shape and
endpoint fitted to that complement, which change with every prefix, and
$\log(1-z/\ahat)$ does not decompose into prefix sums. An exact sweep
costs $\Theta(N^2d_g)$. Fitting the tail once per hypothesis, to the
upper tail of all residual coordinates, makes $\log p(\br_i)$ a fixed
per-observation term and restores a $\Theta(Nd_g)$ sweep. This evaluates a different prefix objective because every prefix uses
the same outlier density.

\paragraph{Soft labels.}
The tail is fitted once per hypothesis, in $\Theta(Nd_g)$ by selection
and a radix sort of the excesses, and no sort of the distances is
needed. The integral over $J$ cells of $\log\sigma$ and $M$ cells of
$\gamma$ costs $\Theta(NJM)$, a factor $JM$ above the sweep. Grid cells can be evaluated independently.

including \gau and \magsacpp for comparison. Per-prefix generalized

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Synthetic validation}
\label{sec:synthetic}

\paragraph{Design.}
Each trial fixes one hypothesis and draws $N=\syncase{uniform}{N}$
scalar residual magnitudes ($d_g=1$, $n_\theta=0$). Inliers follow
$|\NN(0,1)|$. The reference setting has \syncase{uniform}{nin} inliers
and uniform outliers on $[0,A]$, with $A=\syncase{uniform}{halfwidth}$.
Three variants isolate rare inliers (\syncase{uniform_3pct}{percent}\%),
heavy overlap ($A=\syncase{uniform_A10}{halfwidth}$), and a linearly
decreasing outlier density ($A=\syncase{linear}{halfwidth}$). In the
mixed setting, each trial draws a uniform or linearly decreasing shape
and a log-uniform extent on
$[\syncase{mixed}{halfwidthmin},\syncase{mixed}{halfwidthmax}]$.
All methods use the same \SynTrials{} draws per setting. We report F1
against the generated labels and the number selected, denoted $k^*$
for both inference methods.

\paragraph{Comparisons.}
The known-width baseline uses each trial's $A$. The calibrated baseline
selects one of \SynGridN{} log-spaced half-widths between \SynGridMin{} and \SynGridMax{}
by maximizing mean F1 on the evaluation trials. It is an in-sample
reference for that grid. We compare block estimates (\SynBlocks{} maxima or
minima) and peaks over threshold ($\eta=0.8,0.9,0.95$). For the latter
we compare both outlier components, hard and soft labels, and fixed
($\gamma=\tfrac12$) and integrated mixing probabilities.
\Cref{tab:synthetic} reports $\eta=0.9$.

\paragraph{Endpoint estimation.}
With $\gamma=\tfrac12$, the fitted and known half-widths give similar classifications. For example, in the uniform setting both select
\syn{uniform}{half}{pot90-uniform}{k} observations on average, and
across the \SynNumCases{} settings their F1 scores differ by at most
\SynMaxGapKnownEV. Moving $\eta$ from 0.8 to 0.95 changes the F1 score by at
most \SynMaxGapEta. The
median fitted shapes are \syn{uniform}{half}{pot90-uniform}{shape} for

minima collapse in every setting to

preserved the selected partition in \SynInvIdentical{} of
\SynInvTrials{} trials, for both components and every label prior.

\paragraph{Calibration at fixed inlier probability.}
With $\gamma=\tfrac12$, calibration selects a half-width below $A$ in
every fixed-extent setting. The largest discrepancy occurs at
$\syncase{uniform_3pct}{percent}\%$ inliers. The known half-width selects
\syn{uniform_3pct}{half}{fixed-uniform}{k} observations for
\syncase{uniform_3pct}{nin} true inliers (F1
\syn{uniform_3pct}{half}{fixed-uniform}{f1}). The calibrated half-width
of \syn{uniform_3pct}{half}{calibrated-uniform}{halfwidth} raises F1 to
\syn{uniform_3pct}{half}{calibrated-uniform}{f1}. The density-level
equivalence in \cref{eq:gamma-width} predicts much of this effect.
In the reference setting it gives an effective half-width of
\syncase{uniform}{equivwidth}, compared with the calibrated value of
\syn{uniform}{half}{calibrated-uniform}{halfwidth}. For linearly
decreasing outliers, matching the density at zero first gives a uniform
half-width of $A/2$. Adjusting for the inlier probability then gives
\syncase{linear}{equivwidth}, compared with
\syn{linear}{half}{calibrated-uniform}{halfwidth}.

\paragraph{Integrating over the inlier probability.}
With uniform outliers and an integrated inlier probability, the calibrated half-width is
\syn{uniform}{marginal}{calibrated-uniform}{halfwidth} for $A=\syncase{uniform}{halfwidth}$, and the
estimated half-width reaches F1
\syn{uniform}{marginal}{pot90-uniform}{f1} against the calibrated
\syn{uniform}{marginal}{calibrated-uniform}{f1}. For linearly decreasing
outliers the calibrated half-width moves to
\syn{linear}{marginal}{calibrated-uniform}{halfwidth}, close to the half-width $A/2$ that matches the density of the absolute outlier coordinate at zero, $2/A$. At $\syncase{uniform_3pct}{percent}\%$ inliers the F1
score of the estimated half-width rises from
\syn{uniform_3pct}{half}{pot90-uniform}{f1} to
\syn{uniform_3pct}{marginal}{pot90-uniform}{f1}, but the partition now
undershoots, at \syn{uniform_3pct}{marginal}{pot90-uniform}{k}
observations for \syncase{uniform_3pct}{nin} inliers. Under heavy overlap, hard assignments become unstable. At $A=\syncase{uniform_A10}{halfwidth}$ the F1 score falls from
\syn{uniform_A10}{half}{pot90-uniform}{f1} to
\syn{uniform_A10}{marginal}{pot90-uniform}{f1}, and the standard
deviation of $k^*$ grows to
\syn{uniform_A10}{marginal}{pot90-uniform}{ksd}. With $\gamma$ free, a narrow Gaussian can explain a small core of the dense cloud while the remaining observations receive outlier labels.
Mean F1 also falls in the mixed setting (
\syn{mixed}{half}{pot90-uniform}{f1} to
\syn{mixed}{marginal}{pot90-uniform}{f1}). Under hard labels,
profiling $\gamma$ selected the same partition as marginalizing it in
\SynProfileSame{} of \SynProfilePairs{} paired cases.

\paragraph{Fitting outlier shape with hard labels.}
For linearly decreasing outliers and fixed $\gamma=\tfrac12$, fitting the shape gives an F1 of
\syn{linear}{half}{pot90-gpd}{f1}, against
\syn{linear}{half}{pot90-uniform}{f1} for the uniform component. It
ties in the uniform setting (\syn{uniform}{half}{pot90-gpd}{f1} against
\syn{uniform}{half}{pot90-uniform}{f1}) and loses where its extra
parameter costs variance: at $\syncase{uniform_3pct}{percent}\%$ inliers and at $A=\syncase{uniform_A10}{halfwidth}$ the standard
deviation of $k^*$ grows \SynSdRatioMin- to \SynSdRatioMax-fold. In the settings with a single extent it does not reach the calibrated
width. In the mixed setting, every per-trial
estimate (block maxima \syn{mixed}{half}{block_max20-uniform}{f1}, \stuUev
\syn{mixed}{half}{pot90-uniform}{f1}, \stuGP
\syn{mixed}{half}{pot90-gpd}{f1}) edges out the single in-sample
calibrated width (\syn{mixed}{half}{calibrated-uniform}{f1}): adapting
to each trial helps, but only slightly.

\paragraph{Soft labels.}
Integrating over $\gamma$ and summing over labels improves mean F1
relative to hard labels with the same prior in the heavy-overlap
setting. For uniform outliers, F1 increases from
\syn{uniform_A10}{marginal}{pot90-uniform}{f1} to
\syn{uniform_A10}{marginal}{soft90-uniform}{f1}, compared with
\syn{uniform_A10}{half}{calibrated-uniform}{f1} for the calibrated
hard-label baseline. With rare inliers, the soft uniform model scores
\syn{uniform_3pct}{marginal}{soft90-uniform}{f1} when $\gamma$ is
integrated out and \syn{uniform_3pct}{half}{soft90-uniform}{f1} when
$\gamma=\tfrac12$. These comparisons also reflect the change from
per-prefix to per-hypothesis tail fitting.

When shape and extent vary across trials, the soft generalized Pareto
model gives the highest mean F1, \syn{mixed}{marginal}{soft90-gpd}{f1}.
The soft uniform model gives \syn{mixed}{marginal}{soft90-uniform}{f1}
under the same prior, and the calibrated hard-label baseline gives
\syn{mixed}{half}{calibrated-uniform}{f1}. The standard deviation of
the selected count falls from \syn{mixed}{marginal}{soft90-uniform}{ksd}
to \syn{mixed}{marginal}{soft90-gpd}{ksd} when the shape is fitted.
The uniform component performs better when few inliers are available
or the populations overlap heavily. We therefore retain both outlier
components for the hypothesis-ranking experiment. The synthetic
trials hold the hypothesis fixed and use scalar residuals with no fitted
parameters; ranking competing hypotheses, the cost of fitting
$\btheta$, codimension $d_g=2$ and structured outliers are left to the
real pools.

\subsection{Real-data evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:real}

The synthetic experiment holds the hypothesis fixed. The real-data
question is whether estimating the outlier width changes the ranking
of competing hypotheses. The planned comparison uses the stored
hypothesis pools of~\cite{Pritts2026}: essential matrices on
\phototourism~\cite{Jin2021}, scored by the tangent Sampson
error~\cite{Terekhov2023}, fundamental matrices on \ethd~\cite{Schoeps2017},
and homographies on \heb~\cite{Barath2023}. Scoring the same hypotheses
with every method isolates model selection from sampling and from local
optimization~\cite{Chum2003,Lebeda2012}, which a complete
pipeline~\cite{Barath2025superansac} adds.

Within the uniform model, we will compare a cross-validated half-width,
an image-extent half-width, and a fitted endpoint at one quantile level
across datasets. Using the largest coordinate magnitude as a fourth
estimate isolates the contribution of extrapolating beyond the observed
maximum. A second comparison will evaluate both soft-label outlier
models under the same prior on $\gamma$. External baselines are
\acransac, \magsacpp and \simfitpp. We will report the geometric error
of the selected hypothesis, sensitivity to $\eta$ and $a$, and scoring
time including the tail fit. On \heb, correspondences on competing
planes provide a test with structured outliers. We will report fitted
shapes and the frequency of endpoint fallbacks for each dataset.
