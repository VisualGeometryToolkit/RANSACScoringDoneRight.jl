\subsection{Synthetic validation}
\label{sec:synthetic}

We first isolate the estimate of the half-width on one candidate model with
known ground truth.  Each trial draws $300$ inlier distances
$|\NN(0, 1)|$ and $700$ outlier distances uniform on $[0, A]$ with
$A = 100$, so the true half-width is $a = A$; the codimension is $d_g = 1$,
the prior is Jeffreys, and we report the selected prefix length~$k^*$
(mean $\pm$ standard deviation over $100$--$200$ trials) against the true
inlier count.  \Cref{tab:synthetic} compares the fixed true half-width with
three estimates from the complement.

\paragraph{The estimate matches the true half-width.}
In every setting, peaks over threshold selects the same partition as the
true half-width, and it does so without knowing it.  The overshoot of all
columns beyond the true inlier count, and the failures with $3\%$ inliers
and with dense outliers, are properties of the \gaussunif score at the
correct half-width, not of the estimate.

\paragraph{Sensitivity: quantile versus half-width.}
Varying the threshold quantile from $0.8$ to $0.95$ moves the selected
prefix by at most $2$ of $1000$ points.  Varying the fixed half-width over
two decades, $a/A \in \{0.05, 0.5, 5\}$, moves it from $242 \pm 11$ to
$317 \pm 5$ to $329 \pm 7$.  \TODO{a matched sweep: eta and a/A over the same
relative range, with the curves of \cref{tab:synthetic} as a figure}

\paragraph{Diagnostic and invariance.}
The fitted shape is near $-1$ for uniform outliers and $-0.48$ for a
linearly decaying outlier density, against the theoretical $-\tfrac12$.
Rescaling every residual by $1000$ leaves the selected partition identical
in all $30$ trials, as \cref{prop:invariance} states.

\paragraph{Minima collapse.}
Replacing the endpoint by the mean of block minima of the complement
selects the smallest feasible partition, $k^* \approx 3$, in every setting,
including $5$ to $80$ blocks and a margin of $3\hat s$ beyond the boundary
(\cref{sec:why-maxima}).
