\section{Deployment: Hyperparameter, Runtime, and Complete Pipelines}
\label{sec:supp-deploy}

The main experiments isolate the scoring function on fixed candidate
pools.  This appendix collects the evidence needed to deploy it:
how to set the remaining hyperparameter, its cost, and its behavior
inside a complete \ransac pipeline.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The Remaining Hyperparameter}
\label{sec:supp-deploy-a}

\paragraph{Inlier-scale-free, not hyperparameter-free.}
The proposed scores remove the inlier scale~$\sigma$; they do not
remove every hyperparameter.  The outlier half-width~$a$ remains.  It
is an outlier-\emph{domain} parameter, not a scale proxy: we
normalize image coordinates so that the half-diagonal is one, which
fixes the outlier domain in the canonical frame and admits a
dataset-independent default for~$a$ \camtodo{state the default value
of $a$}, after which the model is de-normalized.  Normalization does
not determine~$\sigma$.  Varying~$a$ several-fold has negligible
effect (\Cref{fig:profile-grid}), and on \phototourism and \ethd the
default lies in the flat region.

\subsection{Inference-Time Reliability}
\label{sec:supp-deploy-reliability}

The selected partition provides $k^\star$ and hence $\nu^\star =
k^\star d_g - n_\theta$ at inference.  Under the Jeffreys prior the
posterior of the inlier scale is
$\sigma^2 \mid \cI^\star, \br \sim
\IG\bigl(\nu^\star/2,\, \RSS_{\cI^\star}/2\bigr)$
(\eqref{eq:ig-posterior} at $\alpha_0 = \beta_0 = 0$), whose relative
spread is of order $\sqrt{2/\nu^\star}$.  Thus $\nu^\star$ directly
controls the uncertainty of the inferred scale: a small $\nu^\star$
gives a diffuse posterior and signals that Jeffreys is relying on too
little residual evidence.  This is the regime observed on \heb, where
small inlier sets make Jeffreys less accurate and more variable and
Jeffreys' failures are concentrated on such pairs, while \igrobust
regularizes the posterior scale (\Cref{par:pseudo-obs}).  A
large~$\nu^\star$ indicates a concentrated scale posterior under the
assumed model, but does not by itself detect structured outliers or
model misspecification.

\subsection{Runtime}
\label{sec:supp-deploy-runtime}

\Cref{tab:runtime-hyp} reports single-threaded per-hypothesis
timings.  All scorers receive identical precomputed residuals.  For
\magsacpp we use the lookup-table implementation prescribed by the
paper and shipped with the reference code, its most optimistic
faithful runtime; the closed form is included for completeness.
Although comparison sorting is $O(N\log N)$, our implementation sorts
with a radix sort on 32-bit float keys, so the \sortsweep is
$\Theta(N)$: increasing the correspondence count by $2.21\times$
increases the runtime by $2.24\times$, against the $2.48\times$
that $N\log N$ scaling would predict.  The absolute difference to \msac is
$7.8$--$16.6\,\mu$s per hypothesis.

\subsection{Composition with Local Optimization and Termination}
\label{sec:supp-deploy-lo}

The \sortsweep supplies every quantity the downstream components
of \ransac need: the selected support~$\cI^\star$, its cutoff
$\tau^\star = r_{(k^\star)}$, the inlier ratio $\hat w = k^\star/N$,
and the partition scale $\hat\sigma^2 = \RSS_{\cI^\star}/\nu^\star$.
Standard confidence-based termination uses
\begin{equation}
	K = \frac{\log(1-p)}{\log(1-\hat w^{\,m})},
\end{equation}
and local optimization uses either~$\tau^\star$ or a
partition-anchored width $c = \kappa\,\hat\sigma$, with only the
dimensionless~$\kappa$ cross-validated.  No calibrated residual
threshold is required at test time.

\Cref{tab:lo-anchor} isolates this effect with the same \poselib
truncated Levenberg--Marquardt optimizer~\cite{PoseLib}.  The fixed
variant uses a calibrated threshold~$\tau_{\mathrm{ref}}$, perturbed
at test time by~$\zeta$; the anchored variant re-estimates
$\tau = \kappa\hat\sigma$ for each hypothesis.  Both are tuned at
$\zeta = 1$.

\subsection{A Complete \ransac Pipeline}
\label{sec:supp-deploy-e2e}

We compare our full estimator---the proposed score, with the
estimated partition calibrating the scale of local optimization at
test time---with source-built \superansac~\cite{Barath2025superansac}.
In the tested configuration \superansac uses \prosac sampling, \sprt
verification, terminal local optimization, final least-squares
polishing and its default \magsac scoring, so the baseline is not a
plain-threshold pipeline: it embeds \magsacpp scoring in a highly
engineered public estimator.  Its \texttt{inlier\_threshold}
specifies the \magsac scale bound~$\bar\sigma$, equivalently
$\tau = \kappa\bar\sigma$, and is perturbed by~$\zeta$ in the same
role-faithful manner as in the scoring experiment.

Both methods use matched termination settings (minimum 100 and
maximum 10\,000 iterations, confidence 0.999).  We evaluate 11
\phototourism test scenes with 50 \rootsift pairs per scene and
approximately 4\,000 correspondences per pair; both methods are tuned
once at $\zeta = 1$ on 10 disjoint validation scenes, and $\zeta$
perturbs only their residual-unit hyperparameters at test time.
Accuracy is reported as median pose error and as
\begin{equation}
	\mathrm{mAA}@10^\circ = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{1}{N_s}
	\sum_{i=1}^{N_s}\max\bigl(0,\, 1 - e_{si}/10^\circ\bigr),
	\label{eq:maa}
\end{equation}
where $e_{si} = \max(\Delta R_{si}, \Delta t_{si})$ is the pose error
of pair~$i$ in scene~$s$; scenes are weighted equally and failures
contribute zero~\cite{Jin2021}.

At correct calibration ours improves both accuracy metrics ($1.56^\circ$
versus $2.48^\circ$ median error, 0.697 versus 0.636 mAA) at
$1.3\times$ the wall-clock time.  Under miscalibration ours remains
more accurate at every~$\zeta$ and becomes faster for $\zeta \geq 4$:
scoring quality and termination matter more than isolated
per-hypothesis cost.  The trend matches the scoring-only results:
\magsacpp degrades substantially when its scale bound is miscalibrated.

\subsection{Miscalibration Through a Common Decision Boundary}
\label{sec:supp-deploy-common}

The Profile experiment (\Cref{sec:profile}) perturbs each method's
native hyperparameter, and $\tau$ and~$a$ have different statistical
roles.  We therefore also compare the methods through a common induced
decision boundary.  Let $k$ denote the boundary radius in units of
the assumed inlier scale.  Threshold-based scores use
$\tau = k\hat\sigma$, while our \gaussunif model with
$\gamma = \tfrac12$ gives
\begin{equation}
	a(k, \hat\sigma) = \sqrt{\pi/2}\;\hat\sigma\,
	\exp\!\bigl(k^2/(2d_g)\bigr),
\end{equation}
the inverse of the implicit threshold of \Cref{sec:score}.  Both maps
are homogeneous, $m(k, \zeta\hat\sigma) = \zeta\,m(k, \hat\sigma)$.
Each method is tuned over the same dimensionless $k$-grid at correct
calibration and then frozen; test-time miscalibration changes only
the common scale, $\hat\sigma \to \zeta\hat\sigma$, inducing
$\tau \to \zeta\tau$ and $a \to \zeta a$.  Every method thus receives
the same multiplicative calibration error in its own
parameterization.  The mapping is used only for this controlled
comparison: generatively, $a$ remains an outlier-domain parameter.
The experiment uses identical hypothesis pools, no local
optimization, and 22\,000 test pairs from 11 scenes disjoint from
tuning; mAA is~\eqref{eq:maa}.

All methods tie at correct calibration (0.61--0.62 mAA) but separate
under both deflation and inflation of the scale.  At $\zeta = 16$ our
score retains 0.56 mAA versus 0.14--0.30 for the baselines; at
$\zeta = 128$ it retains 0.45 versus 0.03--0.05.  Threshold-based
scores consume the corrupted scale directly as their inlier boundary,
whereas our score re-estimates the inlier scale for each hypothesis,
exposing only its outlier model to the perturbation.  \magsacpp is
evaluated with its original score, not its approximate \gau
equivalent.  The hypothesis budget of 1000 minimal samples per pair
suffices for every scorer: on 8\,800 PT-E-SIFT-4K pairs from four
held-out scenes, all scorers tie at correct calibration
(mAA@$10^\circ$ 0.534--0.542, median error $2.80^\circ$--$2.96^\circ$),
and under $32\times$ miscalibration ours retains 0.466 mAA and
$4.60^\circ$ while the baselines fall to 0.10--0.14 mAA and
$20^\circ$--$25^\circ$.
