\section{Scoring Runtime}
\label{sec:supp-deploy}
\label{sec:supp-deploy-runtime}

\Cref{tab:runtime-hyp} reports single-threaded per-hypothesis
timings on \phototourism.  All scorers receive identical precomputed residuals.  For
\magsacpp we use the lookup-table implementation prescribed by the
paper and shipped with the reference code, its most optimistic
faithful runtime; the closed form is included for completeness.
Although comparison sorting is $O(N\log N)$, our implementation sorts
with a radix sort on 32-bit float keys, so the \sortsweep is
$\Theta(N)$: increasing the correspondence count by $2.21\times$
increases the runtime by $2.24\times$, against the $2.48\times$
that $N\log N$ scaling would predict.  Our score costs $8.8$--$19.7\,\mu$s more per hypothesis than \msac
and $7.8$--$16.6\,\mu$s more than the \magsacpp lookup table.

These microbenchmarks are not end-to-end slowdowns.  A complete
\ransac iteration also includes minimal solving, residual evaluation
and local optimization, and the termination criterion determines how
many times all of these costs are paid.  \Cref{sec:supp-deploy-e2e}
therefore measures a complete pipeline.

\section{Scale Miscalibration}
\label{sec:supp-miscal}

The main experiments isolate the scoring function on fixed candidate
pools with each method's hyperparameter tuned on the data. This section
measures how the scores (\Cref{sec:supp-scoring}), and a complete \ransac
pipeline built on ours (\Cref{sec:supp-deploy-e2e}), degrade when the
user-supplied scale is wrong.

\paragraph{Scale corruption.}
Methods are tuned once at calibration. At test time, each user-supplied
quantity expressed in residual units is multiplied by
$\zeta\in[1/128,128]$ (deflation for $\zeta<1$, inflation for $\zeta>1$);
dimensionless multipliers and scales recomputed from the selected partition
are unchanged. Thus $\zeta$ measures sensitivity to external calibration
rather than perturbing every internal variable identically.

\paragraph{Metric.}
Unlike the main experiments, which report the orientation error
(\Cref{sec:eval-protocol}), this section reports the pose error, which also
includes the translation direction. For relative pose, we evaluate the four
essential-matrix decompositions and report the smallest maximum angular
error~\cite{Jin2021}. We define
mAA@$10^\circ$ as
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\mathrm{mAA}@10^\circ
		 & =\frac{1}{10^\circ}\int_0^{10^\circ}
		P(e_{\mathrm{pose}}\le t)\,dt,                                 \\
		\text{s.t.}\quad e_{\mathrm{pose}}
		 & =\min_{j\in\{1,2,3,4\}}\max\bigl(e_R^{(j)},e_t^{(j)}\bigr).
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:maa}
\end{equation}
Here $e_R^{(j)}$ and $e_t^{(j)}$ are the rotation and
translation-direction errors of decomposition~$j$. In~\eqref{eq:maa},
$t$ is the pose-error threshold, and $P(e_{\mathrm{pose}}\le t)$ is the
per-scene fraction of pairs whose pose error is at most~$t$. We compute
mAA per scene and then average over scenes, with failed estimates
contributing zero.

\paragraph{Parameter mapping.}
\label{sec:supp-scale-mapping}

The shared scoring coordinate $q\in[1,3.3]$ and the validation reference
scale~$\hat\sigma$, the square root of the median per-pair noise
variance~$\hat\sigma_p^2$ (\Cref{sec:supp-prior-estimation}) over the
validation pairs, determine each score's native residual-unit parameter;
$q$ plays the role of the quantile in $\tau = q\sigma$ (\Cref{sec:intro}).
\ransac, \msac, \simfitpp, and \gau use a threshold or cutoff
$\tau=q\hat\sigma$. \magsacpp uses the upper noise-scale bound
$\bar\sigma=q\hat\sigma$, not a residual cutoff. For the profile-marginal
score, the outlier half-width is
\begin{equation}
	a(q;\hat\sigma,d_g)=\hat\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}
	\exp\!\left(\frac{q^2}{2d_g}\right),
	\label{eq:native-scale-maps}
\end{equation}
which equates the isotropic Gaussian inlier density and uniform outlier
density at residual norm $q\hat\sigma$ under equal assignment priors, the
inverse of the implicit threshold~\eqref{eq:implicit-threshold}. This is a
calibration map, not the exact boundary of the profile-marginal partition;
generatively, $a$ remains an outlier-domain parameter.

Replacing $\hat\sigma$ by $\zeta\hat\sigma$ at fixed $q^*$ multiplies each
native parameter by~$\zeta$:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\tau(q^*;\zeta\hat\sigma)          & =\zeta\tau(q^*;\hat\sigma),             \\
		\bar\sigma(q^*;\zeta\hat\sigma)    & =\zeta\bar\sigma(q^*;\hat\sigma),       \\
		a(q^*;\zeta\hat\sigma,d_g)         & =\zeta a(q^*;\hat\sigma,d_g).
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:native-scale-corruption}
\end{equation}
The scoring-only experiment (\Cref{sec:supp-scoring}) applies the nearest
evaluation-grid value to every test pair without retuning. Inverting the
maps at the original reference scale~$\hat\sigma$ gives the equivalent
coordinates
\begin{equation}
	q_{\tau}^{\prime}=q_{\bar\sigma}^{\prime}=\zeta q^*,
	\qquad
	(q_a^{\prime})^2=(q^*)^2+2d_g\log\zeta,
	\label{eq:corrupted-cutoff}
\end{equation}
where $q_a^{\prime}$ is real only when the right-hand side is nonnegative.

\subsection{Scoring}
\label{sec:supp-scoring}

This experiment evaluates how well each score selects a model under scale
miscalibration, without local optimization: each score ranks the identical
pool of minimal-sample hypotheses of each image pair, and the selected
hypothesis is evaluated directly. Both experiments of this section use a
separate set of \phototourism pools: the 21 scenes are split by scene, not by
pair as in \Cref{sec:experiments}, into \PTValScenes{} validation and
\PTTestScenes{} test scenes of $2\,200$ pairs each, with \rootsift
correspondences capped at $4\,096$ and $4\,000$ minimal-sample hypotheses per
pair. Ours is the
profile-marginal score with the Jeffreys prior. Cross-validation uses only
the shared scoring window $q\in[1,3.3]$, a dimensionless multiple of the
validation reference scale~$\hat\sigma$ (\Cref{sec:supp-scale-mapping}). At
test time the supplied scale is multiplied by $\zeta\in[1/128,128]$ with the
tuned coordinate~$q^*$ held fixed; each score's parameter follows through
its map (\Cref{sec:supp-scale-mapping}) and is used for all pairs without
retuning. For the profile-marginal score, the affected parameter is the
outlier half-width~$a$; the selected partition and its variance
estimate~$\hat s^2$ remain data-dependent. \simfitpp~\cite{Edstedt2025} is
\msac with a data-driven threshold: starting from an initial threshold, it
alternates fitting on a random half of the correspondences with a
bias-corrected median scale estimate on the other half and scores at the
converged threshold; its initial threshold is cross-validated on the~$\tau$
axis like the other threshold-based scores, and the estimate falls back to
it when the iteration leaves the admissible range. Because that range is the
reference's $0.25$--$8$\,px window, \simfitpp scores the pool's residuals
re-expressed in pixels through the stored image normalizations; every other
score works in the normalized frame, where all of its parameters are
defined. The shaded confidence intervals in \cref{fig:supp-sel-miscal} use a
scene-clustered BCa bootstrap~\cite{Efron1987}.

At calibration ($\zeta=1$) the scores are nearly tied
(\cref{fig:supp-sel-miscal}): the profile-marginal score and \simfitpp reach
median errors of $\SelStuMedCal^\circ$ and $\SelSimfitMedCal^\circ$ and mAA
of $\SelStuMAACal$ and $\SelSimfitMAACal$. At $128\times$ inflation, the % reproduce: literal-ok (128x the scale inflation zeta, not \ConvRatioPT)
profile-marginal score reaches a median error of $\SelStuMedInfl^\circ$ and
$\SelStuMAAInfl$ mAA, while \simfitpp reaches $\SelSimfitMedInfl^\circ$ and
$\SelSimfitMAAInfl$ mAA; the other threshold-based scores have median
errors of $\SelThreshMedInflLo^\circ$--$\SelThreshMedInflHi^\circ$ and at
most $\SelThreshMAAInfl$ mAA. The profile-marginal score is thus
substantially more stable under scale corruption. This reflects both its
data-dependent partition selection and scale estimation; by
\eqref{eq:corrupted-cutoff}, the equivalent coordinate of the half-width also
changes only with~$\log\zeta$, $(q_a')^2 = (q^*)^2 + 2d_g\log\zeta$, and the
experiment does not isolate these effects. Re-estimating the threshold per pair (\simfitpp) does not
remove the dependence on the supplied scale: at $128\times$ inflation its % reproduce: literal-ok (128x the scale inflation zeta, not \ConvRatioPT)
median error of $\SelSimfitMedInfl^\circ$ is close to that of the other
threshold-based scores, against $\SelStuMedInfl^\circ$ for the
profile-marginal score.

These pools hold more hypotheses than the scores need: the budget of 1000
minimal samples per pair used in \Cref{sec:experiments} suffices for every
scorer: on 8\,800 \phototourism pairs with about
4\,000 \rootsift correspondences each, from four held-out scenes, all scorers tie at correct calibration
(mAA@$10^\circ$ 0.534--0.542, median error $2.80^\circ$--$2.96^\circ$),
and under $32\times$ miscalibration ours retains 0.466 mAA and
$4.60^\circ$ while the baselines fall to 0.10--0.14 mAA and
$20^\circ$--$25^\circ$.

\subsection{End-to-End \ransac}
\label{sec:supp-deploy-e2e}

The deployed pipeline (Ours) combines the profile-marginal score with
local optimization whose loss width is anchored to the selected partition
(\Cref{sec:supp-deploy-lo}). We compare it with
\superansac~\cite{Barath2025superansac}, including sampling and adaptive
termination, on the \PTTestScenes{} \phototourism test scenes
(\Cref{tab:e2e}). Both are tuned at calibration; at test time, $\zeta$
multiplies each system's residual-unit parameter. At calibration, ours
reduces the median pose error from $\EtoESRMed^\circ$ to
$\EtoEOursMed^\circ$ and raises mAA@$10^\circ$ from $\EtoESRMAA$ to
$\EtoEOursMAA$. Under corruption, our median error stays within
$\EtoEOursRange^\circ$, whereas \superansac's reaches $\EtoESRRangeHi^\circ$
under deflation and $\EtoESRInflMed^\circ$ at $128\times$ inflation. We % reproduce: literal-ok (128x the scale inflation zeta, not \ConvRatioPT)
postulate that the calibrated gain has the same origin: a threshold tuned
on validation pairs is effectively miscalibrated for test pairs whose noise
scale lies outside the validation distribution, whereas partition anchoring
estimates the scale per pair.

\section{Local Optimization and Termination}
\label{sec:supp-deploy-lo}

The \sortsweep supplies every quantity the downstream components
of \ransac need: the selected support~$\cI^\star$, its cutoff
$\tau^\star = \delta_{(k^\star)}$, the inlier ratio $\hat w = k^\star/N$,
and the variance estimate~$\hat s^2$ of \Cref{sec:score}.
Standard confidence-based termination stops after
\begin{equation}
	K = \frac{\log(1-p)}{\log(1-\hat w^{\,m})}
\end{equation}
minimal samples, for a confidence~$p$ that an all-inlier sample has been
drawn, and local optimization uses either~$\tau^\star$ or a
partition-anchored width $\lambda\hat s$, with only the
dimensionless~$\lambda$ cross-validated.  No calibrated residual
threshold is required at test time.

\Cref{tab:lo-anchor} isolates this effect with the same \poselib
truncated-least-squares Levenberg--Marquardt optimizer (TLS-LM)~\cite{PoseLib},
on the \phototourism pools of \Cref{sec:supp-miscal}.  The fixed
variant uses a calibrated threshold~$\tau_{\mathrm{ref}}$, perturbed
at test time by~$\zeta$; the anchored variant re-estimates
$\tau = \lambda\hat s$ for each hypothesis.  Both are tuned at
$\zeta = 1$.
