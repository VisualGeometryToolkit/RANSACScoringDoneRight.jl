Many computer-vision systems, from 3D mapping to augmented reality and robot navigation, need to work out how a camera moved between two photos. They do this by matching points across the images, but many of those matches are wrong. The standard tool for coping with wrong matches, called RANSAC, tries many candidate answers and keeps the one that the most matches agree with. To decide whether a match "agrees", it uses a tolerance that the user has to set by hand. This tolerance stands in for how noisy the good matches are, which is unknown and varies from one application to the next. Set it too tight or too loose and the results get much worse, and tuning it well needs many examples with known correct answers. We replace this fixed, hand-set tolerance with an adaptive one: for every candidate answer, our score estimates the noise level of the good matches from the data and sets its own tolerance accordingly, using a classical statistical calculation that can be done exactly and quickly. The one setting that remains matters far less. On about 70,000 image pairs, the method is far less sensitive to how it is configured than existing approaches, and it reaches near-best accuracy when tuned on just two examples with known correct answers, where existing methods need over a hundred times more.
